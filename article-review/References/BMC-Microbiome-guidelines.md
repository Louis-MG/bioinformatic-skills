## Journal Overview

### About BMC Microbiome

*BMC Microbiome* (ISSN: 2049-2618) is a leading open access journal published by BioMed Central (part of Springer Nature). The journal serves as a forum for presenting the latest research and methodologies in microbiome science, fostering collaboration across environmental, animal, and biomedical microbiome research communities.

### Journal Scope

*Microbiome* encompasses studies of microbiomes colonizing:
- Humans
- Animals
- Plants
- The environment (built, natural, or manipulated such as in agriculture)

**Priority Areas:**
- Development and application of **meta-omics approaches**
- Novel **bioinformatics tools**
- Community/host interaction with emphasis on **structure-function relationships**
- Studies that **mechanistically support proposed microbiome functions**
- Establishment of **cause and effect relationships**

**Not Considered:**
- Studies of individual microbial isolates/species in vivo or in laboratory cultures **without exploring mechanisms** by which they affect complex microbiome structures and functions
- Unsolicited Reviews (journal does not consider these)

**Collaboration:**
- Works closely with sister journals: *Environmental Microbiome*, *Animal Microbiome*, and *BMC Microbiology*
- Manuscript transfers to appropriate sister journals may be offered

### Aims and Objectives

The journal aims to **integrate researchers** with common scientific objectives across a broad cross-section of sub-disciplines within microbial ecology, promoting:
- Cross-disciplinary collaboration
- Methodological innovation
- Theoretical advances
- Mechanistic understanding of microbiome functions

### Publishing Model

- **Open Access**: All articles freely available to everyone
- **No subscription charges or registration barriers** for readers
- **Benefits of Open Access**:
  - 1.6x more citations than non-OA articles
  - 4x more downloads than non-OA articles
  - 2.5x more Altmetric attention
  - 1.9x more news mentions
  - 1.2x more policy mentions
- **Compliance**: Enables compliance with major funder policies internationally

## Original Article (Research Article) Requirements

### Article Type Specification

**Research Articles** in *BMC Microbiome* are reports of new research findings that make a **significant contribution to knowledge** in microbiome science. The journal especially welcomes studies that:
- Go beyond descriptive omics surveys
- Include experimental or theoretical approaches
- Mechanistically support proposed microbiome functions
- Establish cause and effect relationships

### Important Notes

**Before Submission - Read Carefully:**
- [Aims & Scope](https://microbiomejournal.biomedcentral.com/journal/40168/aims-and-scope)
- [A blueprint for contemporary studies of microbiomes](https://microbiomejournal.biomedcentral.com/article/10.1186/s40168-025-02091-0) (Editorial)
- [Submission Guidelines - Research Article](https://microbiomejournal.biomedcentral.com/submission-guidelines/research-article)
- **Data availability policies** (particularly important for Microbiome)

### Article Limits and Requirements

*Specific word count and figure limits for Research Articles are not explicitly stated on the main guidelines page, but the journal follows standard BMC formatting guidelines. Refer to the [Research Article submission guidelines](https://microbiomejournal.biomedcentral.com/submission-guidelines/research-article) for exact specifications.*

**General BMC Research Article Standards:**
- Typical length: No strict word limit, but conciseness is encouraged
- Figures/Tables: No strict limit, but should be appropriate for the study
- References: No strict limit
- **Mandatory**: Availability of data and materials statement

**Note**: Unlike some journals, BMC Microbiome does **not** have rigid word/figure limits for Research Articles, focusing instead on scientific quality and completeness.

### Required Sections

Research Articles must include the following standard sections:

1. **Title Page**
   - Title
   - Authors
   - Affiliations
   - Corresponding author

2. **Abstract**
   - Structured format recommended

3. **Background**
4. **Methods**
5. **Results**
6. **Discussions**
7. **Conclusions**
8. **Declarations** (Mandatory section including):
   - Availability of data and materials
   - Competing interests
   - Funding
   - Authors' contributions
   - Acknowledgements

9. **References**

## Formatting Guidelines

### General Formatting

**Quick Points:**
- Use **double-line spacing**
- Include **line and page numbering**
- Use **SI units**
- **Embed** all special characters in text (otherwise lost during PDF conversion)
- Do **not** use page breaks in manuscript

### File Formats

**Acceptable for Main Manuscript:**
- Microsoft Word (DOC, DOCX)
- Rich Text Format (RTF)
- TeX/LaTeX

**Important Notes:**
- **Editable files required** for production processing
- If manuscript contains non-editable files (PDFs), will be required to re-submit editable file upon revision or after acceptance
- **LaTeX Users**:
  - Encouraged to use [Springer Nature LaTeX template](https://www.springernature.com/gp/authors/campaigns/latex-author-support)
  - PDF compiled during submission using pdfLaTeX and TexLive 2021
  - **All editable source files must be uploaded** during submission
  - Failing to submit source files causes delays in production

### Manuscript Structure

#### Title Page

- **Title**: Clear, descriptive, concise
- **Authors**: Full names of all authors
- **Affiliations**: Institutional affiliations for each author
- **Corresponding Author**: Clearly indicated with email address

#### Abstract

- **Format**: Typically structured (Background, Methods, Results, Conclusions)
- **Length**: No strict limit, but should be concise and informative
- **Content**: Summarize objectives, methods, key findings, and significance

#### Main Text

**Standard Sections:**
1. **Background**
   - Introduce the research context
   - State the objectives/hypotheses
   - Summarize relevant literature

2. **Methods**
   - Describe study design and methodology
   - Include sufficient detail for reproducibility
   - Reference established methods briefly
   - Include ethics approval statements
   - Statistical methods should be clearly described

3. **Results**
   - Present findings clearly and logically
   - Use subheadings as appropriate
   - Tables and figures should be cited in order

4. **Discussions**
   - Interpret the findings
   - Relate to previous research
   - Discuss limitations
   - State conclusions and implications

5. **Conclusions**
   - Summarize main findings
   - Highlight significance and impact

#### Declarations Section (MANDATORY)

Must include:

1. **Availability of data and materials** (Mandatory for all Research Articles)
2. **Competing interests**
3. **Funding**
4. **Authors' contributions**
5. **Acknowledgements** (optional)

#### References

**Guidance:**
- See [BMC Editorial Policies - Citations](https://microbiomejournal.biomedcentral.com/brands/bmc/editorial-policies#Citations)
- Check specific article type guidelines for examples

**What Should Be Cited:**
- Articles published or in press
- Clinical trial registration records
- Abstracts available through public e-print/preprint servers

**What Should NOT Be in Reference List:**
- Unpublished abstracts
- Unpublished data
- Personal communications

**How to Handle Unpublished Material:**
- May be included in text as "unpublished observations" or "personal communications"
- Must give names of involved researchers
- **Responsibility**: Obtain permission to quote from cited colleagues
- Only footnotes permitted for such citations

**Journal Abbreviations:**
- Follow Index Medicus/MEDLINE standards

**In-Press Articles:**
- May be cited if necessary for reviewer assessment
- Must be made available if requested by editorial office

**Web Links and URLs:**
- Give a reference number
- Include in reference list (not in text)
- Provide in full: site title, URL, and access date
- **Format**: "The Mouse Tumor Biology Database. https://tumor.informatics.jax.org/mtbwi/index.do. Accessed 20 May 2013."

**Reference Management:**
- Use of reference management software encouraged

### Figures and Illustrations

**General Requirements:**
- Numbered in order first mentioned in text
- Uploaded in this order
- Multi-panel figures: Submit as **single composite file** containing all parts
- Uploaded in correct orientation
- Figure titles and legends in **main manuscript**, not in graphic file
- Figure keys incorporated into graphic, not legend
- Each figure closely cropped to minimize white space
- **Individual figure files should not exceed 10 MB**

**Figure File Types Accepted:**
- EPS (diagrams/images)
- PDF (diagrams/images)
- Microsoft Word (diagrams/images, single page only)
- PowerPoint (diagrams/images, single page only)
- TIFF (images)
- JPEG (photographic images)
- PNG (images)
- BMP (images)
- CDX (ChemDraw - molecular structures)

**Figure Size and Resolution:**

**Web Version:**
- Width: 600 pixels (standard) or 1200 pixels (high resolution)

**Final PDF Version:**
- Half page width: 85 mm
- Full page width: 170 mm
- Maximum height (figure + legend): 225 mm
- Resolution: ~300 dpi at final size

**Design Requirements:**
- All information (including text) must be legible at specified dimensions
- All lines should be wider than 0.25 pt
- All fonts must be embedded

**File Compression:**
- Vector figures: Submit as PDF (more compact than EPS)
- TIFF files: Save with LZW compression (lossless)
- JPEG files: Save at maximum quality
- Minimize conversion between file types (especially lossy formats like JPEG) to avoid quality degradation

**Copyright:**
- Authors responsible for obtaining permission from copyright holder to reproduce previously published figures/tables
- For open access: Must have permission from rights holder for images published elsewhere in non-open access journals
- **Indicate permission in figure legend**
- **Include original source in reference list**

**Questions:** Contact [info@biomedcentral.com](mailto:info@biomedcentral.com)

### Tables

**Formatting:**
- Numbered and cited in text in sequence (Table 1, Table 2, etc.)
- Tables <1 A4/Letter page: Can be placed within manuscript at appropriate location
- Tables >1 A4/Letter page: Place at end of document, with citation indicating placement
- Large datasets/too wide tables: Upload as **additional files**

**File Formats for Large Tables:**
- Excel spreadsheet (.xls)
- Comma-separated values (.csv)
- Use standard file extensions

**Table Elements:**
- Table titles (max 15 words): Above the table
- Legends (max 300 words): Below the table
- **Do not** embed as figures or spreadsheet files
- Format using 'Table object' function in word processor
- **Color and shading may NOT be used**
- Highlighting: Use superscript, numbering, lettering, symbols, or bold text
- Explain highlighting in table legend
- **Do not** use commas for numerical values

**Questions:** Contact [info@biomedcentral.com](mailto:info@biomedcentral.com)

### Additional Files (Supplementary Material)

**Purpose:**
- Length and quantity of data not restricted for Research Articles
- Can include: datasets, tables, movies, or other information

**Publication:**
- All additional files **published with accepted article**
- **Do not include**: patient consent forms, certificates of language editing, revised manuscripts with tracked changes
- Such files should be sent by email to journal's editorial address if requested

**Requirements:**
- **Maximum file size**: 20 MB each
- Files will be **virus-scanned** on submission
- Each additional file must be **cited in sequence** within main text

**If Including Additional Files:**
- List in separate section of manuscript text:
  - File name (e.g., Additional file 1)
  - File format with extension (e.g., .pdf, .xls, .txt, .pptx)
  - Title of data
  - Description of data

**Citation Format:**
- Reference explicitly by file name in article body
- Example: "An additional movie file shows this in more detail [see Additional file 1]"

**Data Policy:**
- Results indicated as "data not shown" should be included as additional files
- Supporting data must be included as additional files or deposited in recognized repository
- **Do not** link to data on personal/departmental websites
- **Do not** include individual participant details

**Guidance:** [How to use additional files](https://www.springernature.com/gp/authors/campaigns/writing-a-manuscript)

## Journal Overview

### About The ISME Journal

*The ISME Journal* is the flagship publishing venue for the [International Society for Microbial Ecology (ISME)](https://www.isme-microbes.org/). The journal is:

- **Peer-reviewed** and **fully open access**
- Published on a **continuous publication model** (papers published as ready into a single yearly issue)
- Focused on **microbial ecology** with a broad scope covering:
  - Climate change microbiology
  - Microbial contributions to biogeochemical cycles
  - Community assembly
  - Engineered and synthetic microbial communities
  - Host-microbiome interactions
  - Microbiomes of engineered environments
  - Modeling and ecological theory
  - Novel microorganisms and metabolic functions
  - Spatial and temporal dynamics

## Original Article Requirements

### Article Type Specification

**Original Articles** are substantial, in-depth, novel research studies representing major advances for the field of microbial ecology. While the journal emphasizes discovery-based research, breakthrough method papers representing major advances may also be considered.

### Word Count and Limits

| Element | Maximum Limit |
|---------|---------------|
| Main text (excluding tables, figures, references) | 5,000 words |
| Abstract | 250 words |
| Keywords | 10 (minimum: 3) |
| References | 100 |
| Tables or Figures | 8 |

### Required Sections (in exact order)

1. **Introduction**
   - Assume reader is knowledgeable in the field
   - Be brief but may include short historical review where desirable
   - Clearly state the research objectives

2. **Materials and Methods**
   - Contain sufficient detail and cited references for reproducibility
   - Briefly describe previously published methods (do not copy/paste from other publications)
   - Include manufacturer names and locations for specialized equipment
   - Identify drugs by pharmaceutical names (and trade names if relevant)

3. **Results**
   - Briefly present experimental data in text, tables, or figures
   - Do not extensively describe tables/figures in text
   - Support all statements about findings with data displays in brackets
   - Avoid "Figure X shows" statements

4. **Discussion**
   - Focus on interpretation and significance of findings
   - Provide concise objective comments relating to other work
   - Do not repeat information from Results
   - Final paragraph should highlight main conclusion and future research directions

**Note:** Results and Discussion may be combined into one section if appropriate.

## Manuscript Structure

### Title Page Requirements

- **Main title**: Brief, informative, ≤150 characters (including spaces)
  - Should concisely state the main discovery without emphasizing methodology
- **Short title**: ≤40 characters (for running head)
- **Author names**: Full names of all authors
- **Affiliations**: Complete institutional affiliations for each author
- **Corresponding author**: Mailing address and email address

### Abstract

- **Format**: Unstructured (no headings)
- **Length**: Maximum 250 words
- **Language**: English
- **Content**: Summarize essential features in logical, concise sequence
- **Restrictions**: No reference citations, no abbreviations

### Graphical Abstract (Optional)

- Submit as separate file (e.g., `graphical_abstract.tiff` or `featured_image.tiff`)
- Should clearly summarize the focus and findings
- Published as part of the article online and in PDF
- Colour encouraged
- Minimal textual statements
- **Alternative**: If not provided, Figure 1 will be used by default

### Keywords

- Minimum: 3
- Maximum: 10
- Should reflect the main topics and findings

## Ethical and Technical Requirements

### Authorship

- Limited to those who made **significant contributions** to design and execution
- **Ghost authorship** (unnamed author preparing article) is prohibited
- **Gift authorship** (listing non-contributors) is prohibited
- Follows [ICMJE definitions](https://www.icmje.org/recommendations/browse/roles-and-responsibilities/defining-the-role-of-authors-and-contributors.html)

**Authorship Changes After Submission:**
- Require editor approval
- All co-authors must agree
- Disputes must be resolved before acceptance
- Corresponding author changes to access Read and Publish funding are **not permissible**

### Conflict of Interest

- **Required**: Disclosure at submission point
- Include in cover letter AND in a separate "Conflicts of interest" section at end of manuscript
- Corresponding author responsible for ensuring all authors' conflicts are declared
- Follows [COPE guidance](https://doi.org/10.24318/cope.2019.2.6)

**Definition**: A conflict exists when an individual's position, activities, or relationships (financial or non-financial, direct or indirect) could influence or appear to influence their opinions or activities.

### ORCID iDs

- **Encouraged** for all authors
- Register for free at [ORCID website](https://orcid.org/)
- Included in published papers (HTML and PDF versions)
- Can be linked to submission system account

### CRediT

- Uses **Contributor Roles Taxonomy (CRediT)**
- Authors may select roles from [CRediT website](https://credit.niso.org/)
- Multiple roles per author allowed
- Non-author contributors should be listed with contributions described
- Changes require editor approval after submission

### Data Availability

**Requirement**: Mandatory for all Original Articles and Brief Communications

**Policy:**
- All primary and secondary data must be publicly released (where ethically possible)
- Must be available to reviewers during peer review
- Include a **data availability statement** in the paper
- Data should have DOI and be cited in reference list

**Acceptable Data Locations:**
- Main manuscript
- Additional supporting files
- Public repository (e.g., Figshare, Zenodo)

**Microbial Strains:**
- Must be deposited before submission to open culture collection
- Must be made publicly available without restrictions

**Exceptions:**
- May be granted for legal, privacy, ethical, or other limitations
- Request at submission
- If granted, data availability statement must explain what cannot be shared and why

**Recommended Repositories by Data Type:**

| Data Type | Recommended Repositories |
|-----------|-------------------------|
| Protein sequences | [UniProt](http://www.ebi.ac.uk/uniprot) |
| DNA and RNA sequences | [GenBank](http://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/genbank/), [DDBJ](http://www.ddbj.nig.ac.jp/), [ENA](http://www.ebi.ac.uk/ena/) |
| DNA and RNA sequencing data | [NCBI Trace Archive](http://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/Traces/home/), [NCBI SRA](http://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/sra) |
| Genetic polymorphisms | [dbSNP](http://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/snp), [dbVar](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/dbvar/), [EVA](https://www.ebi.ac.uk/eva/) |
| Linked genotype and phenotype data | [dbGaP](http://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/gap), [EGA](http://www.ebi.ac.uk/ega/) |
| Macromolecular structure | [wwPDB](http://wwpdb.org/), [BMRB](http://www.bmrb.wisc.edu/), [EMDB](http://www.emdatabank.org/) |
| Microarray data (MIAME compliant) | [GEO](http://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/geo/), [ArrayExpress](https://www.ebi.ac.uk/arrayexpress/) |
| Crystallographic data | [Cambridge Structural Database](http://www.ccdc.cam.ac.uk/) |

**Data Availability Statement Examples:**
1. "The datasets generated during and/or analysed during the current study are available in the [NAME] repository, [PERSISTENT WEB LINK TO DATASETS]."
2. "The datasets generated during and/or analysed during the current study are not publicly available due [REASON] but are available from the corresponding author on reasonable request."
3. "Data sharing not applicable to this article as no datasets were generated or analysed during the current study."
4. "All data generated or analysed during this study are included in this published article [and its supplementary information files]."
5. "The data that support the findings of this study are available from [third party name] but restrictions apply... Data are however available from the authors upon reasonable request and with permission of [third party name]."

## Formatting Guidelines

### General Style

- Follow **Oxford SciMed style**
- Use **UK spelling** throughout
- Use **Oxford commas** (serial commas)
- Exceptions: quotations and references may retain original style
- [Style Guide Reference](https://static.primary.prod.gcms.the-infra.com/static/site/journals/document/mini-style-checklist-oxford-scimed-numbered.docx)

### Tables

- Use only for **essential data** (do not duplicate text)
- Number sequentially (Table 1, Table 2, etc.)
- Reference all tables in text
- Submit in **editable format** (e.g., Microsoft Word), not as images
- Place at **end of main text**
- Avoid excessive formatting (colour, shading, tabbed spacing)
- Explain formatting/superscripts in footnotes
- Provide units in column/row headers (not in body)
- Like data should read **down** (not across)
- Tables should not be included within figures

### References

**General:**
- At submission: any readable style acceptable
- If author/date system used: may be queried for resupply
- Authors responsible for accuracy

**Style:**
- Follow **New Hart's Rules/Oxford SciMed numbered references**
- List in **numerical order**

**Formats:**

**Journal Article:**
```
Kennedy T, Jones R. Effect of obesity on esophageal transit. Am J Surg 1985;149:177-81.
```
- Include full DOI link where available

**Book:**
```
Long HC, Blatt MA, Higgins MC, et al. Medical Decision Making. Boston: Butterworth-Heinemann, 1997.
```

**Book Chapter:**
```
Manners T, Jones R, Riley M. Relationship of overweight to haitus hernia and reflux oesophagitis. In: Newman W (ed.), The Obesity Conundrum. Amsterdam: Elsevier Science, 1997, 352-74.
```

**Website:**
```
Public Health Laboratory Service. Antimicrobial Resistance in 2000: England and Wales. http://www.hpa.org.uk./infections/topics_az/antimicrobial_resistance/amr.pdf (7 January 2004, date last accessed).
```

**Additional Formatting:**
- 4+ authors: list first three followed by "et al."
- Page spans: elide (e.g., 213-5, 137-87, 1185-2042)
- Avoid "ibid"
- In-text citations: numbers in square brackets before punctuation [1, 3-5]
